- type: cards
  background: light
  anchorId: challenges
  eyebrow: The geospatial challenges
  heading: Six challenges of a work management system
  items:
  - tag: '1'
    title: Thousands of items, changing all day
    text: Work items are created, assigned, started and closed continuously. A map that reloads everything on every change is unusable. Updates must be cheap and visible quickly.
  - tag: '2'
    title: Work belongs to assets, not only addresses
    text: A job sits on a pole, a cable, a main or a meter, often described in several systems with different quality. Each item needs a reliable location and a link to the network.
  - tag: '3'
    title: Readable from region to street
    text: Thousands of points must stay legible in the overview and detailed at street level, with status and priority visible, without choking the browser.
  - tag: '4'
    title: Spatial questions, answered fast
    text: Which urgent gas jobs are open within a few kilometres of this crew, due today, needing a certain skill? Space, attributes and time must combine at interactive speed, for many users at once.
  - tag: '5'
    title: Travel time, not straight lines
    text: Real travel depends on one-way streets, restrictions, access and road type. Travel times between many crews and many work locations are needed again and again.
  - tag: '6'
    title: The best plan, recomputed quickly
    text: Assigning and sequencing jobs must respect skills, shifts and priorities, put emergency work first, and adapt when the day changes. It has to take seconds, not hours.
- type: cards
  background: white
  anchorId: geocoding-challenges
  eyebrow: Geocoding
  heading: Why address matching is harder than it looks
  intro: A work management system starts many jobs from an address, a customer call or a parcel number. Turning that text into a reliable place on the map is geocoding, and it has its own set of problems.
  items:
  - tag: '1'
    title: Messy input
    text: 'People type addresses in every way: abbreviations, different word order, typos, missing accents, house numbers like 12/A or 12-14, parcel numbers. The text must be normalised before it can be matched.'
  - tag: '2'
    title: The same name in many places
    text: A street name exists in many settlements and districts, and streets get renamed. Without context the right match is a guess.
  - tag: '3'
    title: No perfect address list exists
    text: Official address data, the company's customer and meter addresses and its asset locations disagree. New buildings, missing house numbers, rural places without a street address, plots known only by parcel number.
  - tag: '4'
    title: How sure is the match?
    text: Exact, approximate and ambiguous matches must be told apart. A confidence score and ranked candidates let the system accept good matches automatically and send doubtful ones to a person.
  - tag: '5'
    title: House, street or settlement?
    text: A result can be a building, a point on the street, an interpolated position or just a village. A crew needs to know which, and where the access point is.
  - tag: '6'
    title: Speed, scale and privacy
    text: Search as you type must answer in milliseconds, bulk matching must handle large lists, and customer addresses are personal data, which makes sending them to an external geocoding service problematic.
- type: split
  background: light
  anchorId: asset-registry
  eyebrow: Asset data
  heading: A complex asset registry, kept in sync
  body: '<p>The network assets live in a registry database built for record keeping, with complicated relations and many states. We took the data over from it, and synchronise it to the work management system on a regular basis.</p><p>That is harder than copying tables:</p><ul><li><strong>Relations:</strong> assets connect in hierarchies and networks, such as supply points, feeders, transformers, cables, mains, service lines and meters, and a job often concerns an asset and everything connected to it</li><li><strong>States:</strong> assets are planned, in operation, switched off, out of service or retired, with history, and those states must be translated into what matters for work</li><li><strong>One source of truth:</strong> the registry stays the master. The work management system holds a working copy that must not drift from it</li><li><strong>Changes and deletions:</strong> only what changed should be carried over, in the right order, without loading the production registry</li><li><strong>Data quality:</strong> broken relations, missing locations and odd states exist in every registry, so exceptions are reported instead of hidden</li></ul><p>The same discipline as in our <a href="/ai-etl">ETL work</a> applies: repeatable, traceable, and checked against the source.</p>'

- type: cards
  background: light
  anchorId: solution
  eyebrow: The solution
  heading: Built on open source geospatial technology
  items:
  - tag: PostgreSQL + PostGIS
    title: One spatial source of truth
    text: Work items, assets and the road network live in one spatial database. Spatial indexes make questions like "what is open, urgent and near here" interactive.
    link: /technology#database
  - tag: Vector tiles
    title: A live map that stays light
    text: The map is served as vector tiles generated from PostGIS. Thousands of points draw smoothly, styling by status and priority happens in the browser, and changes reach the map shortly after they happen.
    link: /technology#services
  - tag: MapLibre GL JS
    title: The dispatcher's map
    text: A fast WebGL map with clustering in the overview, detail at street level, filters by status, type, crew and date, and selection tools for asking questions about an area.
    link: /technology#frontend
  - tag: pgRouting
    title: Routing on the real network
    text: Travel routes and times are computed on the road network inside the database, next to the data. Travel-time matrices between crews and work locations feed the optimisation.
    link: /technology#database
  - tag: Optimisation
    title: Assign and sequence the work
    text: From the open work, the crews and the travel times, the module proposes assignments and the order of visits, shown on the same map, and recomputes when the day's work changes.
  - tag: Geocoder
    title: Address search and matching
    text: The geocoding module normalises address text, matches it against reference address data and returns ranked candidates with a confidence score. It serves search as you type and batch matching of work items.
    link: /technology#database
  - tag: ETL
    title: Registry synchronisation
    text: 'Asset data is taken over from the registry database and synchronised to the system regularly: relations and states are translated, changes carried over, and exceptions reported.'
    link: /ai-etl
