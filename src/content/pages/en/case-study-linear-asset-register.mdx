- type: cards
  background: light
  anchorId: concepts
  eyebrow: Concepts in plain language
  heading: Four ideas this project is built on
  items:
  - tag: '1'
    title: Linear facility
    text: A road, a railway, a pipeline or a power line is long and thin. It has an identity, a start and an end, connections to other lines, and many properties that change along its length. A register records what exists, where, in what state and who is responsible. Budgets, maintenance, safety and legal responsibility all start from it.
  - tag: '2'
    title: Linear referencing
    text: 'Locating something by its position along a line instead of by map coordinates: on route X, at kilometre 12+350, 4 metres to the right. That position along the route is the measure (chainage, in Hungarian practice the szelvényszám). Engineers, crews and legacy data speak in kilometres, and a measure stays meaningful when coordinates change with a new survey or projection.'
  - tag: '3'
    title: Objects and phenomena
    text: Things that exist on the line, such as bridges, signs and culverts, are objects. Things that happen or hold along it, such as a speed limit, a pavement condition, roadworks or an incident, are phenomena (events). Both attach to the line by linear reference, as a point or a range. Dozens of independent layers can then be stored once and overlaid on the same route.
  - tag: '4'
    title: Temporal change management
    text: The register must answer not only what is true now, but what was true on a given date and what is planned. Facilities are built, rebuilt, realigned and closed, and facts are also corrected afterwards. Reporting, investment planning, audits and incident investigation all need the state on a specific date.
- type: split
  background: white
  imagePosition: left
  anchorId: linear-referencing
  eyebrow: Linear referencing
  heading: Position along the route, not only on the map
  body: '<p>A route has a measure: the distance from its start, shown on the ground as kilometre markers, where 12+350 means 12 km and 350 m. A point object is stored with one measure, a range event with a start and an end measure, and either can have an offset to the left or right of the route.</p><p>The same route can carry any number of such layers, each with its own breaks, and none has to cut the others into pieces. This is often called dynamic segmentation: the pieces are computed when needed, not stored as fixed segments. One attribute can change without touching the others, data from different sources and years can be overlaid, and field data collected in kilometres is usable as it is.</p><p>The concept is described in the international standard ISO 19148.</p>'
  image: /images/linear-referencing-en.svg
  imageAlt: 'Diagram: a route with kilometre measures, point objects, range events and an incident beside the route'
- type: cards
  background: light
  anchorId: lr-hard
  eyebrow: The difficulty
  heading: What makes linear referencing hard
  items:
  - tag: '1'
    title: Routes change
    text: A realignment or rebuild shortens or lengthens a route, so the measures after the change shift. Every object and event referenced to that route must be re-referenced, or it points to the wrong place.
  - tag: '2'
    title: More than one measure system
    text: The official chainage, the kilometre markers on the ground, rarely equals the real geometric length. Both are needed, linked by calibration points.
  - tag: '3'
    title: Direction, lanes and carriageways
    text: A route can have two directions, separate carriageways and several lanes. Events can apply to one side or both, and left and right are defined relative to the direction.
  - tag: '4'
    title: Branches, overlaps and gaps
    text: Junctions, ramps, one road carrying two route numbers, and stretches where no data exists. The rules for what is valid must be explicit.
  - tag: '5'
    title: Coordinates and measures must agree
    text: A point at measure 350 must lie 350 m along the geometry, within a stated tolerance. Positions from GNSS or survey have to be snapped to the route.
  - tag: '6'
    title: Several networks, one reference
    text: Roads, railways and pipelines each have routes, and an object such as a pipeline crossing a road relates to both. The reference model must allow more than one route per object.

  background: white

  eyebrow: Change over time
  heading: 'Time: what was true on a given date'
  body: '<p>Two kinds of time matter. <strong>Valid time</strong> is when something was true in the real world: the speed limit was 70 from March 2021. <strong>Recording time</strong> is when the system learned about it: a correction entered in 2025 may say that the limit was already 70 in 2021.</p><p>A register that keeps both can answer "what was true on 1 June 2022" and also "what did we believe on 1 June 2022". Facilities also pass through states over their life: planned, under construction, in operation, under reconstruction, out of service, decommissioned. Planned future states must be stored without being mistaken for the present.</p><p>This matters for legal reporting, investigations, audits and investment decisions: a number in a report must be explainable, for a date, years later.</p>'

- type: cards
  background: light
  anchorId: time-hard
  eyebrow: The difficulty
  heading: What makes change management complex
  items:
  - tag: '1'
    title: Geometry and referencing change together
    text: When a route is realigned, its geometry gets a new version and every object and event on it must follow, keeping its meaning in both the old and the new version.
  - tag: '2'
    title: Stable identity
    text: A facility keeps its identity through rebuilds, splits and merges, or its history breaks. The rules for when something is the same thing must be written down.
  - tag: '3'
    title: Real change or correction?
    text: A rebuild is a change in the world, a correction fixes a mistake in the data. They are recorded differently and answer different questions.
  - tag: '4'
    title: Planned, approved, actual
    text: Future states, approval workflows and the moment a plan becomes reality need clear rules and roles.
  - tag: '5'
    title: Queries at any date
    text: Every report, map and interface must accept an as-of date, and stay fast over years of history.
  - tag: '6'
    title: Migrating history
    text: Existing data rarely has a clean history. Deciding what to carry over, and how to mark uncertain dates, is part of the specification.
